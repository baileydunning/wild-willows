Who Wild Willows is for

Wild Willows is rated 9+ on the App Store. Nothing in it is frightening, violent, or commercial — the rating is really about how much reading the game asks for, and there is a setting that changes that.

Why 9+ and not 4+

Apple reviewed Wild Willows and issued it a 9+. The gap between that number and how the game actually plays is almost entirely a reading gap.

What the rating is about

Wild Willows teaches real ecology, and it does that in words. Every animal carries a real-world fact. There is a weather & seasons guide that explains why a superbloom happens and what a food web is. Crafting, habitat requirements and species notes all come with explanations.

By default that writing sits at roughly a 7th-grade reading level. A confident 9-year-old reads it comfortably; a 5-year-old does not.

What it is not about

There is no combat, no enemies, no timers, and no way to fail. Animals are observed and welcomed home — never hunted, harmed, captured, or lost. Nothing dies.

No ads, no purchases inside the game, no chat, no accounts, and no personal information. On content alone, Wild Willows would sit comfortably at 4+.

The short version for a parent: if your child can read, they can play this. If the reading is the problem, turn on Simpler wording in Settings and the entire game — interface, animal notes, ecology writing — rewrites itself in plainer language. Nothing is hidden or removed.

Reading, and the “Simpler wording” setting

Settings → Accessibility → Simpler wording. It can be switched on and off at any time, mid-game, and it applies to the whole game rather than just menus.

Coverage
1,526 English strings and 1,600 Spanish strings have a plain-language version. Anything without one falls back to its normal text, so you never see a gap or a missing line.
How this is measured
Flesch Reading Ease and Flesch–Kincaid Grade Level for English; Fernández Huerta, the Spanish adaptation of Flesch, for Spanish. The comparison is pairwise — the same sentence scored twice, once in each wording — and a build check fails if the plain version ever stops reading easier than the text it replaces.

Take the exact numbers lightly. Readability formulas count syllables and sentence length and know nothing about whether a word is common. The single scores are rough; the gap between the two wordings is the honest part, because it holds the vocabulary and the subject matter fixed.

Simpler wording also pairs with the rest of the reading settings: four text sizes, five interface fonts (including a plain, high-legibility one), a high-contrast theme, and dark mode. All of them live on one Settings screen and all can be changed mid-game.

Online features

The game is fully playable offline. When online, it sends only anonymous gameplay statistics — play time, items crafted, animals returned — so the game can be improved. Nothing personal, and nothing is shown to or shared with other players.

An optional feedback form in Settings sends a message privately to the developer. It is the only free-text input that leaves the device, and including an email address is optional. Full details are in the privacy policy.

If a future update enables the optional co-op mode (web version), players join a shared world only by invite code with the host's explicit approval, and other players see just a chosen caretaker name and character. There is no chat system.

For parents

Wild Willows has no mechanisms that pressure play.

Nothing punishes absence

No timers, no daily-login streaks to lose, no crops that wither while you're at school. A world you leave is exactly where you left it.

Nothing to buy

The full game is a one-time purchase. There is nothing to spend money on inside it, and no advertising.

No strangers

No chat, no friend requests, no profiles, no user-generated content. Nobody can contact your child through this game.

Common questions

Is Wild Willows rated by ESRB or PEGI?

No. Wild Willows is sold on the Mac App Store and on itch.io, and only Apple has issued it a rating: 9+. It has not been submitted to the ESRB or to PEGI, so this page doesn't claim a rating from either — the content descriptions above are the honest version of the same information.

Can a younger child play it?

Yes, with a hand nearby or with Simpler wording turned on. Nothing in the game is unsuitable for a younger player — the only barrier is the amount of text, and Simpler wording, the four text sizes and the five interface fonts all exist to lower it.

Does the browser demo differ?

No, not in content. The free demo at play.wildwillows.app is the same game with less of it — same art, same tone, same absence of ads, purchases and chat.

Does it need an internet connection?

No. The game is fully playable offline, with no account and no sign-in required.

What data does it collect?

Anonymous, aggregate gameplay statistics only, and an optional feedback message if you choose to send one. No accounts, no personal information, no advertising identifiers. The privacy policy lists exactly what and why.

Where do I report something?

Email wildwillowsgame@gmail.com, or use the support page. Feedback from Settings inside the game reaches the same inbox.

See it for yourself

The free browser demo needs no download and no sign-in. It's the fastest way to judge whether it suits your reader.
