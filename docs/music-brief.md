# Wild Willows — Audio Brief

## What the game is

A cozy nature-restoration life sim: you restore a damaged nature preserve biome by biome and welcome wildlife back by gathering, crafting, and planting habitats.

**Audience:** the cozy-game market on the Mac App Store and itch — players who reach for comfort games to unwind, all ages, plays well in the background of an evening. Nature and wildlife lovers are the core; the ecology in the game is real and lightly educational. The audio should feel like something you could leave on for an hour without noticing it.

**Budget: $2,000.** Priority order:

1. **The 7–8 music loops** — the core of the project and the part only you can do. The first two tracks (welcome theme + Willow Meadow) are most important by far. Adaptive tracks are a stretch goal.
2. **SFX are the flex:** ideally you cover the *signature* sounds (animal returns, biome unlock, achievement) so they feel musical and match the score. Everything else (clicks, footsteps, gather, rain, ambience) can be library-sourced separately.

I know this budget is well below your usual rate — it's what the project can support right now, and I've tried to structure the scope so it's a fair trade. If the game grows, revisiting rates for a Phase 2 is the first thing I'd want to do.

Everything below is intended as creative context rather than a rigid checklist. You have full freedom to interpret the direction and suggest what will work best musically. The lists describe the game's broader audio needs, not the full scope expected within the $2,000 budget.

**Proposed Phase 1 (the flat-fee scope):** welcome theme + Willow Meadow loop + the three signature SFX (animal return, biome unlock, achievement), with remaining biome loops added as budget allows. Welcome + meadow covers the entire early game. Adaptive layers, stems, the home interior loop, additional SFX, weather beds, and biome ambience are optional or future additions depending on what fits comfortably. If the game grows and it makes sense down the line, I'd love to revisit the wishlist and expand the audio in a later phase — let's define the final Phase 1 together.

## Music: looping tracks (7–8)

| Track | Damaged state (arrival) | Healthy state (restored) | Likely listening time |
|---|---|---|---|
| Welcome / character creation | — | Inviting theme; sets the cozy tone. | 1–5 min per boot, every boot. |
| Willow Meadow | Dusty, quiet, sparse — grasses stripped, pollinators gone. Thin and still. | Humming with life — light, warm. | **The big one: 20–65 min continuous, often a player's entire playtime.** Everyone starts here; most never leave. |
| Old Hollow Forest | Logged and hollow — big trees gone, understory bare, birds moved on. Open, exposed. | Sheltered and alive — dense canopy, a little mysterious. | First unlock. 30–60 min for players who get here. |
| Rushwater Wetland | Drained and dry — cracked channels, murky standing water, no reeds. | Flowing and full — reed beds, frog chorus, watery reflection. | Mid-game (1–3 hours in). 20–45 min. |
| Redstone Scrubland (desert) | Overgrazed flat — bare, nothing moves. | Living scrubland — cactus bloom, shade and burrows, warm open space. | Late-game. 20–45 min for the dedicated. |
| Graywind Heights (alpine) | Trampled slope — turf worn through, silent talus. Thin, cold. | Alpine turf and snowmelt pools — airy, high, quietly bright. | Late-game. 20–45 min for the dedicated. |
| Pelican Shore (coast) | Scoured coast — washed-out dunes, empty tidepools. Bleak surf. | Anchored dunes, living tidepools, kelp and gulls — breezy, expansive. The finale biome. | Final unlock. 20–45 min; the "you did it" area. |
| Home interior (optional) | Bare tent at camp — sparse, makeshift, just starting out. | Built and upgraded home — cozy, settled, furnished. Can reuse/rework the welcome theme. | Short visits, a few min at a time. |

The damaged→healthy columns are the adaptive stretch goal: one track per biome whose arrangement can read as either state (sparse base layer = damaged; fuller layers = healthy). If adaptive isn't feasible in budget, write for a middle mood that works across both.

Most biome loops can be 60–90 seconds if they sustain repetition well; the meadow may deserve a longer form or alternate variation.

Shared instrument palette across biomes is welcome — keeps the preserve cohesive and the scope sane.

**In-game behavior (how tracks get used):**

- Track changes when the player walks into a different biome — simple crossfade, no musical transition needed.
- Home interior: welcome theme (or variant) plays inside.
- Title theme runs on the menu and character creation; gameplay track starts on entering the world.
- Music may duck or thin at night (ambience takes over) — open to input here.

## Sound effects

**Progression / rewards** (animal arrival is the core reward moment)

- Animal returns (fanfare)
- Biome unlocks (bigger fanfare)
- Achievement earned
- Goal/quest step complete (distinct from achievement — happens far more often, should be smaller)
- Recipe unlocked
- Tool upgraded
- Home built / home upgraded

**Player actions**

- Footsteps — needs surface variants (2–3 round-robins each)
- Gather / pick resource (the single most frequent sound in the game)
- Dig
- Water / pour
- Place object / plant
- Pick up & move a placement
- Craft something (complete)
- Chest open/close
- Discard item (happens a lot — soft, not punishing)
- Observe animal with the field journal (pencil scribble / page note)
- Tool switch (basket / shovel / watering can / field journal on number keys)
- Sleep → wake at dawn (rest transition)

**UI**

- Click / confirm
- Unable to click / invalid action
- Panel or journal open/close
- Text blip (narrative & tutorial lines)

**Weather & ambience (looping beds)**

The game has seven weather types: clear, cloudy, rain, **storm**, fog, snow, dry heat. Each biome rolls its own weather once per in-game day (every 12 real minutes), weighted by biome and season. Clear/cloudy need no bed; the ones that do:

- Rain (any temperate biome, most common in the wetland)
- Storm (heavier rain + wind; can be the rain bed with an intensity layer rather than its own bed — desert storms are a distinct gameplay moment, so a dry-thunder variant is a nice-to-have)
- Fog (muffled, hushed; common in forest, wetland, and coast)
- Snow / cold wind (mostly alpine — near-constant there in winter; light winter snow in meadow/forest/wetland)
- Dry heat (desert only; shimmer, sparse insects)
- Per-biome ambient beds — meadow insects/birdsong, forest birds/creaks, wetland frogs/water, desert wind, alpine wind, coastal surf & gulls (these can be library-sourced to save budget; day/night variation — crickets, owls — can live here instead of in the music)

**Variation note:** anything triggered constantly (gather, footsteps, click) needs 3+ round-robin variations or it will grate over hours of play.

## Listening patterns — from recent playtest data (17 players)

**The meadow track carries the whole game.** Every player starts in the meadow, and in playtesting over the past week only 1 of 17 ever unlocked a second biome. Players sit in the meadow for 20–60+ minutes continuously — real single sessions of 36, 45, and 65 minutes, all in the meadow. Minimum realistic exposure is ~20 min; an engaged first-timer hears it for an hour straight with no scene change.

What that means musically:

- **The meadow track must survive extreme repetition** — the single most important requirement. Low ear-fatigue: gentle dynamics, no attention-grabbing hook that repeats every 60 seconds, nothing shrill or insistent. Background consistency matters more than a memorable melody here.
- Worth making the meadow loop the longest and most varied track (or 2 variations the game can alternate) even if the other biomes get shorter loops — budget-wise the meadow deserves a double share.
- Later biomes are earned hours in, so they're heard less and can be simpler.
- The title theme is heard ~1–5 min per boot — it can afford to be more melodic/memorable.
- The game world changes around the music: a full day/night cycle (night → dawn → day → dusk) every 12 real minutes, weather rerolling per biome at each day rollover (also every 12 min), and a season change (spring → summer → autumn → winter, each with its own weather odds and visual tint) every 36 minutes. Ambience beds tied to those shifts can provide the variety the music loop doesn't have to.

**SFX repetition is even more extreme.** Playtesters averaged ~12 gathers/minute while active; the most engaged player hit 48 actions/minute. Gather, footsteps, and terraform sounds fire thousands of times per session (6,800+ gathers across 9.6 total playtest hours) — they need to be short, soft-attack, and have 3+ round-robin variations, or they'll be muted within the hour.

**Session shape:** avg session 22 min; engaged players 45–65 min. Cozy, low-stakes pacing — no combat, no fail states. The animal-arrival moment is the game's emotional payoff. It happened 157 times in testing — several per session for engaged players — so it should feel rewarding but not huge and brassy; a warm, musical flourish that still lands the twentieth time you hear it.

## Technical specs

**The project.** TypeScript web game: React + Vite UI shell, Phaser 3 game world, shipped as an Electron desktop app (Mac App Store/itch; Chromium under the hood). Currently ships with zero asset files (all art is procedurally generated) — audio will be the first real assets, so file size matters.

**Delivery formats**

- Masters: WAV, 48 kHz / 24-bit (music & beds stereo; point SFX mono is fine)
- Build format: OGG Vorbis (~q5–6). OGG loops gaplessly in Phaser; MP3 does not.
- Loops must be sample-accurate seamless — no reverb tail chopped at the loop point (or deliver as intro + marked loop region).

**Mix & loudness**

- Agree a consistent loudness target (e.g. music around −16 to −14 LUFS, SFX peaking below −6 dBFS) so in-game mixing stays simple — no middleware (no FMOD/Wwise; plain Phaser audio).
- Settings menu will have separate music and SFX toggles/volume controls, so every asset must be cleanly categorized as one or the other — ambient/weather beds count as SFX. Nothing baked into both (e.g. no birdsong mixed into a music track — it belongs in the ambience bed so it mutes with SFX, not music).

**Delivery & housekeeping**

- Stems for the biome tracks if possible (enables the adaptive stretch goal later).
- Clear file naming: `music-meadow-loop.ogg`, `sfx-gather-01.ogg`, etc.
- Shared drive delivery; WAVs archived, OGGs go in the build.

## Process

- Playable build provided up front — hearing nothing while playing is the best brief.
- **Checkpoint first:** meadow track (or a 30-sec sketch of it) delivered and approved before the remaining tracks are written, so direction is locked early.
- Delivery order after that: welcome theme + signature SFX, then remaining biomes — welcome + meadow covers the entire early game.
- Timeline: agree a rough schedule and what "done" means per track.
- Agree on revision process.

## Business

- Flat fee for a defined Phase 1 scope (vs. open-ended hourly).
- Payment schedule tied to the direction-lock checkpoint: **$500 up front, $750 on meadow-track approval, $750 on final delivery.**
- **Rights — open to discussion; the options as I understand them:**
  - **Work-for-hire:** I own the copyright outright; you can't reuse the tracks elsewhere without permission. Strongest for the game, biggest ask of you.
  - **Exclusive license:** you keep the copyright; only Wild Willows can use the music. You retain ownership but can't reuse or resell the tracks.
  - **Non-exclusive license:** you keep the copyright and can freely reuse the tracks — portfolio, streaming, sync, whatever. Lightest ask; optionally with a carve-out that the same tracks don't go to another game.
  - Whichever we pick, the license should cover all Wild Willows distribution and promo (itch, Mac App Store, Steam if/when, trailers, ports) so we never have to renegotiate per channel. Soundtrack/streaming release rights and how they interact with each option are worth deciding together.
- Credits: agree on wording and placement (e.g. "Original score by Jon Licht" on the title screen and store pages).
